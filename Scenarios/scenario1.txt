Norie Yamauchi is a 22-year-old female university student in her fourth year, majoring in economics at a public university. She is currently job hunting but has not yet secured a position. She lives alone, having moved away from her parents, and is their only child. Both of her parents work, so neither of them can accompany her on the day of her admission. However, her mother will visit the hospital tomorrow, on September 28, and both parents will be there on the day of her surgery. Her mother plans to take a week off work. While they are financially stable, they cannot afford a private room by choice, but due to the hospital's availability, she was placed in one. Her mother is a nurse.
During middle and high school, Norie was in the gymnastics club, and in university, she became an active member of the cheerleading team. She is known for being proactive and has many friends. She has no known allergies. She drinks alcohol occasionally at social gatherings and does not smoke.
One day in April of her fourth year, while practicing cheerleading, she suddenly experienced severe pain in her lower back that radiated to her right leg. The pain was so intense that she could not attend classes for a week. After seeing a doctor, she was diagnosed with a lumbar disc herniation. Initially, her treatment consisted of medication, including pain relievers and muscle relaxants, while resting. With sedation treatment, the pain would temporarily disappear for a few hours but would gradually return. From around August, her symptoms worsened. She now experiences numbness and pain extending from her lower back down to her legs. Sitting for over an hour becomes unbearable, and she feels pain when transitioning between sitting and standing. Walking for more than 30 minutes causes her lower back to hurt, and she feels a tingling sensation in her legs. Some parts of her legs feel numb to the touch, and lifting her legs with her knees straight is difficult.
Recently, the pain in her back has been so severe that she cannot sleep properly without sedatives, and she only gets about four hours of rest. The pain persists in any position, though she feels some relief when lying on her back with her knees bent and a cushion between them. A nutritionist from her cheerleading team advised her to consume more protein to help with the recovery of her herniated disc, so she has been eating more chicken but generally maintains a balanced diet with three meals a day.
Norie was the captain of the cheerleading team in her third year and had been coaching her juniors in dance and acrobatic techniques. However, she has been on a break since April, and she remains concerned about her juniors. Since then, she has hardly been able to move her body, and she has gained 3 kilograms. She occasionally experienced back pain since her second year of high school, but it was never severe, so she did not pay much attention to it. Looking back, she realizes she may not have taken enough preventive measures, such as stretching, to avoid injury. She frequently fell on her buttocks or hit her lower back during gymnastics practices and sometimes during aerial stunts in university as well. Due to her lumbar disc herniation, she is scheduled for surgery on September 30 and was admitted to the hospital today, September 27.
Currently, she manages her pain with painkillers. This will be her first surgery and hospital stay. Although she has received an explanation of the surgery and preoperative orientation in the outpatient clinic, she feels scared and anxious as she imagines the procedure, the post-operative recovery, and the pain she may experience. There are many things she does not fully understand, which adds to her worries.
